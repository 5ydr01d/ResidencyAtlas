**Disuse atrophy follows**

*Appeared in: Residency Mar 2025 · Set 4 – Pathology · Q1*

*True/False (5 stems)*

- a) Blockage of the duct of an exocrine gland — **F**
- b) Diminished secretion of trophic hormones — **F**
- c) Immobilization of a joint — **T** ✅
- d) Interference with the blood supply — **T** ✅
- e) Interference with the nerve supply to the muscles controlling joint — **T** ✅

**High-yield topic coverage — Atrophy and cell adaptation**
*Topic appeared in: March 2024; Residency March 2025*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Loss of motor innervation causes denervation atrophy; it may occur in muscles controlling an immobilized joint.
- Immobilization reduces mechanical use and protein synthesis, causing disuse atrophy of skeletal muscle.
- Loss of trophic hormones (e.g. Oestrogen in endometrium, androgens in prostate) can cause atrophy.
- Reduced blood supply can cause atrophy if chronic; severe/prolonged ischaemia causes cell injury and necrosis.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- Reduced trophic hormones cause endocrine-dependent atrophy, not disuse atrophy.
- Duct obstruction causes pressure atrophy of the affected gland (and possibly inflammation), not disuse atrophy.
- Overload generally causes hypertrophy, not atrophy.
- Inflammation is not a classic direct cause in the standard atrophy list; chronic inflammation may indirectly cause tissue loss.

*Topic appeared in: March 2024; Residency March 2026; Diploma 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**

**High-yield topic coverage — Granulomatous and chronic inflammation**
*Topic appeared in: March 2024; Diploma 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Epithelioid histiocytes are activated macrophages and the characteristic cell morphology of granulomas.
- Sarcoidosis classically causes noncaseating granulomatous inflammation.

**High-yield topic coverage — Inflammatory mediators, pain and vascular effects**
*Topic appeared in: Residency March 2025; Residency March 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Endothelial nitric oxide raises platelet cGMP and inhibits platelet activation while causing vasodilation.
- Prostaglandins such as PGE₂ and PGI₂ promote vasodilation in acute inflammation.
- Histamine from mast cells causes rapid arteriolar vasodilation and increased venular permeability.
- Prostacyclin (PGI₂) inhibits platelet aggregation via increased platelet cAMP.
- Bradykinin directly activates nociceptors and contributes to inflammatory pain.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- Plasminogen activator promotes fibrinolysis; “plasminogen activating factor” is imprecise wording and is not a standard anti-platelet mediator.
- Cysteinyl leukotrienes (LTC₄/LTD₄/LTE₄) cause bronchoconstriction and permeability; LTB₄ is chemotactic. They are not standard primary vasodilators.
- Thromboxane A₂ is not a major chemotactic mediator; LTB₄ and chemokines are better examples.
- Von Willebrand factor mediates platelet adhesion to exposed subendothelial collagen and supports platelet plug formation.

*Topic appeared in: March 2024; Diploma 2026*

### 4.4 Haemodynamics: Thrombosis, Embolism, Infarction, Shock, Oedema & Effusions
#### Q4.4.1

**The characteristic feature/s of shock is/are**

*Appeared in: March 2024 paper · source question 6*

*True/False (5 stems)*

- a) hypertension — **F**
- b) tachycardia — **T** ✅
- c) fever — **F**
- d) sweating — **T** ✅
- e) syncope — **T** ✅

**High-yield topic coverage — Shock and circulatory failure**
*Topic appeared in: March 2024; Residency March 2026; Diploma 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Syncope can occur from cerebral hypoperfusion in shock.
- Sympathetic activation commonly causes cool clammy sweating in hypovolaemic/cardiogenic shock; distributive shock may differ.
- Severe GI haemorrhage causes reduced intravascular volume, tachycardia, hypotension and cold clammy skin: hypovolaemic shock.
- Compensatory sympathetic activation commonly causes tachycardia.
- Sepsis causes distributive shock through vasodilation, endothelial dysfunction and maldistributed blood flow.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- Fever is not a defining feature of all shock; it may occur in septic shock.
- Shock is usually associated with hypotension; hypertension can occur transiently in some early/neurogenic or catecholamine states but is not characteristic.
- Anaphylaxis causes distributive shock from vasodilation/permeability, not obstructive shock.
- Tension pneumothorax impairs venous return and is obstructive shock.
