#### Q1.1.1

**Ribosome**

*Appeared in: March 2024 paper · source question 1*

**High-yield topic coverage — Ribosome structure and protein synthesis**
*Topic appeared in: March 2024*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- rRNA forms the structural and catalytic core of ribosomes.
- Eukaryotic ribosomes consist of large and small subunits.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- Codons in mRNA specify amino acids; ribosomes translate, not convert amino acids into genetic code.
- Ribosomes synthesize proteins; they do not store them.
- Only a subset of ribosomes bind rough ER.

**Gap junction**

*Appeared in: March 2024 paper · source question 4*

*True/False (5 stems)*

**High-yield topic coverage — Gap junctions and intercellular communication**
*Topic appeared in: March 2024*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- They pass ions and many small metabolites (including some sugars/amino acids); permeability depends on connexin type and molecule size.
- Gap junctions connect neighbouring cytoplasms, allowing direct transfer without entry into extracellular fluid.

- A 6-mm intercellular gap is far too large; gap-junction gaps are only a few nanometres.
- Gap junctions are selective for size, charge and connexin composition.

**Integral membrane proteins**

**High-yield topic coverage — Integral membrane proteins, channels and pumps**
*Topic appeared in: Residency March 2025*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Many integral proteins diffuse laterally within the fluid lipid bilayer, unless anchored or constrained.
- Integral proteins form ion channels, carriers and pumps; selectivity depends on the protein’s structure.

- Individual membrane proteins are below conventional light-microscope resolution; they are studied by electron microscopy, immunostaining or biochemical methods.

#### Q1.1.11

**Golgi apparatus**

*Appeared in: Residency Mar 2025 · Set 1 – Anatomy and applied anatomy · Q17*

*True/False (5 stems)*

- a) Has trans-face through which proteins enter into it — **F**
- b) Has cis-face that is located closest to the rER (endoplasmic reticulum) — **T** ✅
- c) Is well developed in secretory cells — **T** ✅
- d) Produces cellular enzymes — **F**
- e) Stains with hematoxylin and eosin — **F**

**High-yield topic coverage — Golgi apparatus: modification, sorting and lysosome formation**
*Topic appeared in: Residency March 2025; Residency March 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Secretory cells have prominent Golgi for processing, sorting and packaging secreted products.
- The cis face is oriented toward the rough endoplasmic reticulum and receives transport vesicles.
- Golgi enzymes add and remodel carbohydrate groups on proteins and lipids; glycosylation may begin in the ER and be modified in Golgi.
- The Golgi modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles for secretion, membranes or organelles.
- Lysosomal hydrolases are sorted and packaged at the trans-Golgi network into lysosomes/endosomal pathways.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- The Golgi modifies and sorts enzymes, but most cellular proteins are synthesized on ribosomes, not by the Golgi.
- Proteins enter the Golgi at the cis face and are sorted/exited at the trans face; reverse the two faces to make this true.
- The Golgi is often seen as a pale juxtanuclear zone on H&E and is poorly defined by routine staining; specialized stains/electron microscopy show it better.
- ATP is produced chiefly by mitochondria (and glycolysis in cytosol), not by the Golgi.

#### Q1.1.12

**The zygotene stage of the prophase I is characterized by**

*Appeared in: Residency Mar 2025 · Set 1 – Anatomy and applied anatomy · Q18*

*True/False (5 stems)*

- a) Beginning of the close association of the homologous chromosomes — **T** ✅
- b) Condensation of the chromatin — **F**
- c) Formation of a synaptonemal complex — **T** ✅
- d) Formation of chiasmata — **F**
- e) Transposition of DNA strands between the two different chromosomes — **F**

**High-yield topic coverage — Meiosis stages and chromosome number**
*Topic appeared in: Residency March 2025; Residency March 2026; Diploma 2026*
**Core concept and decisive facts**
- Homologous chromosomes begin synapsis during zygotene; this is a defining event of the stage.
- The synaptonemal complex forms as homologues pair during zygotene.
- Primary oocytes remain diploid (46 chromosomes, duplicated chromatids) while arrested in prophase I.
- Primary spermatocytes are diploid (46 chromosomes) before completion of meiosis I.
- Each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids after DNA replication; their longitudinal splitting/recognition is seen during prophase I.
**Important distinctions and associated facts**
- Crossing-over exchanges DNA between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during pachytene; it is not simply strand transposition.
- Chromosomes begin condensing in leptotene, before zygotene.
- Chromosomes condense and appear as long thin threads in leptotene; zygotene/pachytene show progressively paired, thicker chromosomes.
- Spermatids are haploid products of meiosis II; a diploid cell would have 46 chromosomes.
